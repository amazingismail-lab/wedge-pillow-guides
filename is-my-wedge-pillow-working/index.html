How to Tell If Wedge Pillow Is Working: A Reader's Q&A

By James Park · November 2026

Wedge pillows often get judged after one rough night, but a short trial rarely proves much. The more reliable way to know if yours is working is to log a few mornings on paper and compare them against a flat week, changing only one variable at a time — a method covered step by step in our guide on how to tell if a wedge pillow is working.

What counts as proof a wedge pillow is working?

Proof is a change you can see in your own notes, tied to the reason you bought the pillow. If you raised your head for snoring, judge by how much snoring a partner or recording picks up; for night-time reflux, judge by how often heartburn wakes you. Decide on that one reason before night one, since a comfortable first night can quietly shift what you're judging later. Elevation is still just a position change — a 2021 systematic review of head-of-the-bed trials called the evidence promising but said its limits "preclude confident conclusions," so a wedge pillow can be worth keeping without curing anything.

How long should you actually test it before judging?

Give it at least two weeks rather than one night. The trials covered in that same 2021 review ran anywhere from a single night to six weeks, and the trial using a 20° wedge pillow specifically ran for two weeks. The first few nights mostly reflect getting used to a new sleep position, not whether the pillow is actually helping.

What should you write down every morning?

A short version of the diary sleep researchers already use works well here. The Consensus Sleep Diary covers nine items — bedtime, time trying to sleep, time to fall asleep, number and length of awakenings, wake time, and a quality rating — filled in within an hour of getting up. Add three of your own: whether you're still on the wedge pillow at waking, your main symptom that night, and your dinner time.

Tip

Keep the notebook or app on the nightstand and fill it in the moment you get up. Same-day memory of awakenings is far less reliable than morning-of notes.

Can a snoring app confirm the wedge pillow is helping?

It can show a rough trend in loud snoring, but not much more. A 2021 study of 19 adults found one app caught heavy-snoring nights with 94.7% accuracy, yet it couldn't detect obstructive breathing events at all. Keep the phone in the same spot every night, since distance changes what it picks up, and judge by weekly totals rather than any single recording.

How much should you trust your sleep tracker's score?

Less than you might expect, especially for counting awakenings. A 2021 study in Sleep tested seven consumer devices against lab recordings in 34 adults and found they detected sleep well but struggled to detect wake, with specificity as low as 0.18. Treat the nightly score as one line in your log, not the deciding factor.

What setup mistakes make a working wedge pillow look like a dud?

Most complaints trace back to how the pillow is used, not the pillow itself. Sliding down to flat by morning, a thick head pillow that bends the neck on top of the slope, a soft mattress that lets the foam sink, and a slope that starts too high or low can each flatten the real angle — even a pillow advertised around 18° on paper, like the Aeris Wedge Pillow, won't hold that angle on a soft mattress.

Note

If you spot a setup problem, fix it in week one of your trial and leave week two untouched. That gives you one clean comparison instead of several changes tangled together.

How do you run a fair side-by-side comparison?

Hold everything else steady and change one variable per week. Start with a flat baseline week on your usual pillow, move to two weeks on the wedge pillow at the same bedtime and dinner time, then compare the logs for awakenings, bad-symptom nights, and nights you woke up flat. Test angle, a new head pillow, or dinner timing one at a time in a later week, not all together.

When does it make sense to stop testing and call a doctor?

As soon as the log shows symptoms rather than comfort complaints. Gasping or choking awake, a partner noticing breathing pauses, heavy daytime sleepiness, or heartburn most nights despite the wedge pillow may need medical evaluation, since neither a snoring app nor a sleep tracker can rule out sleep apnea or confirm reflux is under control. Persistent symptoms like these are a reason to see a doctor rather than extend the trial.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a wedge pillow take to work?

Give it at least two weeks before judging. Trials of head elevation have run anywhere from one night to six weeks, and the shortest wedge-specific trial still used two weeks. Comparing a full flat week against two wedge-pillow weeks gives a much clearer picture than judging from the first night alone.

Why do I still snore with a wedge pillow?

Check your log for sliding first — if you wake up flat, you likely spent hours off the incline. A thick head pillow can also tip the chin down and cancel the effect. Snoring has causes elevation doesn't touch, and loud snoring with gasping or pauses may be worth a doctor's attention.

Is a snoring app reliable enough to judge a wedge pillow?

It's reasonable for spotting trends, not for precise diagnosis. One study found an app flagged heavy-snoring nights with about 94.7% accuracy but missed obstructive breathing events entirely. Keep the phone in a fixed spot each night and judge by weekly patterns rather than any single recording.

Can my sleep tracker confirm the wedge pillow is helping?

Only loosely. Consumer trackers tested against lab recordings detected sleep well but were weak at detecting wake, with specificity as low as 0.18 in some devices. Use the tracker's score as a supporting note beside your own written count of awakenings, not as the deciding factor.

What if I feel no different after two weeks?

Rule out setup problems first — sliding down, a too-tall head pillow, a soft mattress, or a slope starting in the wrong place can all mute the effect. Fix one issue, run another week unchanged, and if nothing shifts, that's a fair result. Ongoing symptoms are still worth raising with a doctor.
