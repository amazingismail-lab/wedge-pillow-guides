Cinnvoice vs GIVANLEE Mattress Wedge: Your Top Questions Answered

By James Park · September 2026

A mattress wedge is a full-width foam ramp that tilts the head end of the entire bed, which is a different job from a wedge pillow that only tilts one sleeper. The Cinnvoice and GIVANLEE ramps sit in that same full-width category but are shaped very differently, and every figure below comes from each brand's own Amazon listing, read on 18 September 2026.

What makes a mattress wedge different from a wedge pillow?

A mattress wedge is a panel cut to the width of a bed, meant to lift the whole sleeping surface, while a wedge pillow is sized to one person's shoulders and rests on top of the mattress. Because a mattress wedge moves the whole bed, both sleepers on a shared bed rise together instead of one person perched on a smaller incline.

How do the two products compare on size and rise?

The Cinnvoice panel measures 74.8 by 31.5 inches and slopes from a 5-inch head end down to a 1-inch edge over that 31.5-inch run, while the GIVANLEE king version is 76 by 26 by 6 inches. GIVANLEE also sells 12 combinations in total — twin, queen and king widths, each available in 4, 5, 6 and 7.5-inch heights — so there's more room to pick a height that suits you than the four Cinnvoice options (queen and king, in 5 and 7 inches).

How steep is each ramp?

Neither brand states a slope angle, so the closest estimate is rise divided by run: roughly 7 degrees for the Cinnvoice and roughly 13 degrees for the GIVANLEE 6-inch version, assuming the foam tapers in a straight line. That's a meaningfully steeper climb on the GIVANLEE side for a shorter stretch of mattress.

Under the mattress or on top of it — which placement is right?

GIVANLEE's listing offers both positions, saying underneath keeps your existing mattress feel while on top puts you directly against the foam; Cinnvoice's listing title places it under the mattress, though its bullet copy also describes the same piece as a topper. If keeping your mattress surface unchanged matters to you, under-mattress placement leaves nothing on top that can shift or slide during the night.

Tip

Before ordering, measure your mattress width against the stated panel size — both listings carry several size variants on one product page, and it's easy to land on the wrong one.

How much elevation do you actually need?

This is a starting point for comparing specs, not medical guidance, and a doctor is worth consulting if reflux, snoring or breathing symptoms persist despite trying elevation. The NIDDK lists raising the head 6 to 8 inches as one lifestyle step for reflux, and a 2021 review of five trials in 228 patients using bed blocks, a wedge, or both found symptom improvement reported in four of them. The American College of Gastroenterology's 2022 guideline backs head-of-bed elevation for night-time symptoms too, but rates it a conditional recommendation built on low-quality evidence — so it may help, but it isn't guaranteed to.

Does bed size affect which wedge will fit?

Yes — check the stated panel width against your actual mattress, since a queen measures 60 inches and a king 76 inches according to the Better Sleep Council. The Cinnvoice's 74.8-inch panel is king-width regardless of what its listing title says, while GIVANLEE states a matching width for each bed size it sells.

What do the listings leave unanswered?

Both leave out foam density, product weight, a warranty, and any foam certification, and Cinnvoice's own page contradicts itself — its bullets call the fill "high-density foam" while its product overview lists the fill as "soft fabric." Neither maker's listing states the low end height where the GIVANLEE ramp meets the mattress, whereas Cinnvoice is explicit that its low edge sits at 1 inch.

Note

If off-gassing or VOC content matters to you, CertiPUR-US certifies foam free of formaldehyde and heavy metals, and its directory is where you can check whether a brand's foam is listed.

Which one is the better pick?

It depends on what you're optimizing for: the Cinnvoice's longer, shallower slope suits a king mattress where its 1-inch step disappears into the bed, while GIVANLEE's wider size range and stated washable cover suit anyone who wants to dial in an exact height. Frequently asked questions

Is a mattress wedge the same as a wedge pillow?

No. A wedge pillow is sized for one sleeper's upper body and sits on the mattress surface, while a mattress wedge is a bed-width panel placed under or on top of the mattress that tilts the entire head end of the bed. Couples sharing a bed tend to prefer the full-width option since both sides rise evenly.

Which is steeper, the Cinnvoice or the GIVANLEE?

The GIVANLEE 6-inch version is steeper by our arithmetic — about 13 degrees against roughly 7 degrees for the Cinnvoice — because it packs a similar rise into a shorter run. Neither maker publishes an actual angle, so these are estimates based on stated dimensions, not measured figures.

Can a mattress wedge help with snoring?

It may, for some people, since raising the head of the bed is a common suggestion tied to airway position during sleep, but neither listing cites a clinical trial specific to that product. If snoring is frequent or severe, a doctor can help rule out sleep apnea rather than relying on elevation alone.

Does the Cinnvoice or GIVANLEE fit a queen bed?

GIVANLEE lists an explicit 60-inch queen option alongside twin and king. Cinnvoice also lists a queen variant, but the panel dimensions on the listing we read (74.8 inches) match a king width, so it's worth confirming the exact size before ordering.

How long before a rolled mattress wedge lies flat?

GIVANLEE's listing states 3 to 5 days to flatten and suggests steam to speed it up, while Cinnvoice describes unrolling and pressing the foam without giving a specific timeframe. Both arrive compressed for shipping, so expect visible creases for the first few days regardless of which one you choose.
