Wedge Pillow for Tall People: How to Check the Fit

By James Park · November 2026

A wedge pillow that works well for someone 5'6" can leave several inches of a taller sleeper's back without support, because the ramp itself has a fixed length while the body on it does not. Below are the questions tall sleepers raise most often, and what the available data and health guidance say about each one.

Why does a wedge pillow fit tall people differently than shorter sleepers?

Because the slope ends at a set distance from its base, and a taller torso simply runs out of ramp sooner relative to where it needs support. On a shorter frame, a 25-inch slope can reach past the waist; on someone over six feet, the same slope may end around the lower ribs instead. The pillow hasn't changed — the body lying on it has.

Where should the bend in your back fall when you use a wedge pillow?

Ideally at or near your hips, so your chest and head rest on one continuous incline rather than folding partway up your spine. The NHS advises raising the head of the bed itself so that "your chest and head are above your waist," rather than stacking extra pillows, which it warns "can increase pressure on your belly."

Watch out

Piling loose pillows under your head instead of using a true incline may put more pressure on your stomach, which the NHS specifically cautions against for reflux. A wedge that reaches your hips avoids that fold entirely.

How long are standard and extra-long wedge pillows?

Most sleeping wedges run about 24 to 26 inches at the base, while extra-long versions reach roughly 31 inches. The Aeris Wedge Pillow, for instance, sits around 25.6 inches long with a slope closer to 27 inches once you measure the diagonal rather than the base — a useful reminder that the surface you actually lie on is slightly longer than the listed size.

How can you tell if a wedge pillow is long enough for you?

You can check in about two minutes with a tape measure and a willing helper. Lie on your back where you'd normally rest your head, then have someone measure from the top of your shoulder to the crease where your thigh meets your hip. Compare that number to the pillow's slope length (not just its base). If the slope is shorter, expect the bend in your back to land above your hip rather than at it. Tip

If you're only short by an inch or two, try shifting your shoulders up toward the apex and adding a flat pillow on top for your head — that can shift where your hips land without buying a new wedge.

Why do tall sleepers slide down a wedge pillow, and how do you stop it?

Sliding happens partly from gravity and partly from fit: when the ramp ends above your hips instead of at them, there's nothing for your hips to rest against, so your body tends to drift downward overnight. Puffy's practical fixes include anchoring the wedge against a headboard or wall, placing a non-slip mat underneath it, and swapping a silky pillowcase for a textured one. Firmer foam can also help, since foam that compresses under the shoulders may create a dip that encourages sliding.

Is an under-mattress wedge better than a wedge pillow for tall sleepers?

It can be, mainly because it sidesteps the length problem rather than working around it. Lifting the mattress itself tilts the whole torso from the hips, so there's no ramp edge to land on partway up the back — though it requires a flat foundation and typically affects a partner's side of the bed too unless the frame is split. What mattress length do tall sleepers need to make room for a wedge pillow?

Longer than you might assume, since a full-size mattress is only 75 inches and a 31-inch wedge can eat into that noticeably. Mattress makers generally steer sleepers over about 6'2" toward 80-inch beds such as a queen or twin XL, which leaves meaningfully more room for hips, legs, and feet after the wedge is in place.

Frequently asked questions

What size wedge pillow is best for a tall person?

Measure your shoulder-to-hip distance while lying flat, then look for a wedge whose slope length matches or exceeds that measurement. Standard wedges run about 24 to 26 inches, while extra-long versions near 31 inches tend to suit sleepers over roughly six feet better, since they're less likely to end the incline above the hips.

Does a taller wedge pillow help a tall person more?

Not necessarily, because height mainly changes the angle of the slope, not how far it reaches down your back. A steeper wedge on the same base still ends at the same point. If the ramp currently falls short of your hips, more length — or an under-mattress wedge — usually helps more than more height.

Why does my back hurt when I sleep on a wedge pillow?

A common reason is that the slope ends partway up the back instead of at the hips, creating a fold in the wrong place. Checking your shoulder-to-hip measurement against the pillow's slope can confirm this. If the pain is severe or doesn't ease up after adjusting your setup, it's worth discussing with a doctor or physiotherapist.

Can a tall person just use a standard-length wedge pillow?

Often, yes, with small adjustments. Resting your shoulders closer to the apex and using a separate flat pillow for your head can shift more of the slope under your torso. If your hip crease still lands well past the end of the wedge, an extra-long model or an under-mattress wedge is likely to fit better.

How do I stop sliding down my wedge pillow at night?

Start by checking the length fit, since a slope that reaches your hips gives your body something to rest against. After that, pushing the wedge against a headboard, adding a non-slip mat beneath it, and using a textured pillowcase are the steps most often recommended for keeping it in place through the night.
