Wedge Pillow Shared Bed: Does It Disturb Your Partner?

By James Park · October 2026

If you share a mattress with someone who has no interest in sleeping on a slope, a wedge pillow may be the only elevation option that doesn't ask anything of them. Unlike bed risers or an under-mattress wedge, which tilt the entire sleeping surface, a wedge pillow is a foam ramp that rests on top of the mattress on your side only. Here's what the research behind bed elevation actually tested, how much room a wedge takes up, and where couples tend to run into trouble when they try to tilt the whole bed instead.

Does a wedge pillow lift your whole bed or just your half?

Just your half. A wedge pillow is a standalone foam ramp, so the mattress underneath stays flat and only the person lying on the ramp is raised. Bed risers and full-width under-mattress wedges work differently — they change the geometry of the frame or mattress itself, which tilts both sleepers at the same angle whether or not both of them want it.

How much of a shared mattress does a wedge pillow actually take up?

Usually less than half the bed, but it's worth checking the numbers. A queen mattress is 60 inches wide, giving each person about 30 inches of personal space, and a typical sleeping wedge — including the Aeris Wedge Pillow at 65 cm, or about 25.6 inches — leaves roughly four inches of clearance before it reaches the midline. A full-size bed is tighter: at about 27 inches per sleeper, a 24-to-26-inch wedge leaves only an inch or two of margin, and ordinary movement in the night can close that gap. A king, at about 38 inches per side, has room to spare.

Has a wedge pillow itself been tested for reflux, or just whole-bed elevation?

Only whole-bed elevation has been studied directly. In the 2012 Khan trial, patients slept with a 20 cm block under the bed-end legs for six nights, and supine reflux time and the number of long reflux episodes both fell. The larger IBELGA crossover trial used 20 × 20 × 20 cm wooden risers for six weeks and found 69% of patients reached a meaningful improvement in their symptom score. Neither study isolated one sleeper with a pillow — both raised the entire bed.

Tip

The ACG's 2022 guideline mentions bed elevation and sleeping on a wedge in the same sentence, but it rates the recommendation as conditional and based on low-certainty evidence — worth keeping in mind before expecting dramatic results.

Is raising one side of the bed with risers a safer alternative?

Not really, on a frame built for two people. A bed frame is rigid, so lifting legs on just one side tilts the whole platform sideways rather than raising half of it evenly. In IBELGA, over half of participants reported some adverse reaction to the 20 cm tilt, and about a quarter stopped altogether — citing bed instability, breakage, and in one case "sexual problems associated with the tilting of the bed."

Watch out

If you're considering stacking blocks under a queen frame to raise one side only, be aware this load case isn't what the frame or the research was built for.

Will a wedge pillow disturb the person sleeping next to you?

Less than tilting the bed, but not zero. Because the mattress stays flat, your partner's sleeping surface doesn't change, but your raised torso can pull a shared duvet into a ridge along the midline and open a gap on their side. Separate top covers resolve this fairly reliably, which is part of why two-duvet setups are common in some countries regardless of elevation.

What if neither of you wants to touch the bed frame at all?

Sleep position can help without any equipment. The ACG guideline rates left-side sleeping as unequivocal in strength of evidence — stronger than head elevation — and it costs the other sleeper nothing. For reflux specifically, a sham-controlled trial of a positional-therapy wearable found 44% of users cut their nocturnal reflux score by half, against 24% on the sham device, by discouraging right-side sleeping rather than changing the bed's angle. More detail on weighing a one-sided wedge against whole-bed methods is in our wedge pillow shared bed guide.

If reflux, snoring, or disrupted sleep persists despite trying these adjustments, it's worth checking with a doctor to rule out other causes.

Frequently asked questions

Can a wedge pillow fit on just one side of a queen bed?

Yes. A queen gives each sleeper about 30 inches of width, and a typical sleeping wedge is 24 to 26 inches at the base, leaving a few inches of clearance. Centering it on your own half rather than the middle of the headboard helps, and checking the base width — not the apex height — before buying matters more on a full-size bed, where the margin is much tighter.

Does a wedge pillow work as well as raising the whole bed?

No trial has compared the two directly, so there's no clear answer either way. The published research — Khan 2012 and IBELGA — only tested whole-bed elevation, so that method has data behind it. A wedge pillow is reversible and limited to one sleeper, which may matter more if only one of you wants elevation.

Why does my side of the duvet feel short when I use a wedge pillow?

Raising your torso several inches higher than your partner's shoulder level can pull the shared duvet into a ridge along the middle of the bed, leaving a gap on the lower side. This is a common complaint with one-sided elevation. Switching to two separate top covers usually solves it more completely than adjusting the wedge.

Is it safe to tilt a shared bed with risers if only one person wants elevation?

Not easily — a bed frame tilts as one rigid unit, so risers under one side raise the whole surface unevenly rather than lifting just one person's half. In the IBELGA trial, some participants reported frame instability or breakage even with risers placed correctly under both head-end legs, which is the load case the frame was meant to handle.

Can sleep position replace elevation entirely?

For some people, it may help enough on its own. Left-side sleeping is rated unequivocal in evidence strength by the ACG's 2022 guideline, stronger than head elevation, and it doesn't require any equipment or change anything for your partner. It can also be combined with a wedge pillow or whole-bed elevation if symptoms continue.
